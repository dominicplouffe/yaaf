# 05 — A recommended book, reviewed every week

**You want:** a book you can act on, with the case for every change written
down. **You don't want:** an agent anywhere near your brokerage account.

**Import:** `05-portfolio-review-desk.json`.

| | |
|---|---|
| Shape | **Operate** — a weekly cycle, rolling Fridays |
| Teams | Book (`portfolio-manager`), Risk (`risk-manager`), Research (`equity-analyst`) |
| Budget | $300/month · real-world spend capped at **$0** |
| Wakes | CIO 08:00 daily |

## The one rule this example is built around

**The desk recommends. You execute.** It is in the CIO's charter, in the
portfolio manager's prompt, and in the goal text, three times over, because it
is the thing that must not drift. `external_monthly_usd_cap: 0` backs it with
arithmetic: this company cannot spend a cent in the world, at any autonomy
setting, without an amendment you write.

## Risk is a gate, not an opinion

Every recommendation passes the risk manager before it reaches you, and it
returns **APPROVE / APPROVE WITH LIMITS / REJECT** with the numbers behind it.
A rejection is a complete deliverable. If the risk seat approves everything,
it is not doing its job — check its rejection rate at the review.

The thing it exists to catch: five names that are one bet. Correlation over
more than one window, and a sentence naming what the book actually depends on.

## The weekly cycle

Friday's roll closes the cycle, carries unfinished scope forward, reopens the
plan gate, and hands the CIO the last three cycles' narratives verbatim — which
is how last week's mistake reaches this week's plan without you retyping it.

The **Review** tab is where you actually work this project: the platform's
numbers, the CIO's explanation of the variance, and your six levers, each
recorded with your reason and shown back next week beside what it bought.

## What to watch

- **Exits.** A book with no exits is a book nobody is managing. Every holding
  has an invalidation and a review date; ask what got cut and why.
- **Attribution.** Right for the wrong reason is the most dangerous outcome in
  this seat, and the attribution file is where it shows up.
- **Costs.** A rebalance that improves the model by less than it costs is not
  an improvement.
