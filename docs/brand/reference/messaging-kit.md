# yaaf — Voice and messaging kit

## The voice in one line
**The incorruptible chief of staff.** Short, true sentences that lead with the answer. Numbers sit next to what they compare to. Dry, never jokey. Candid about limits.

yaaf already enforces this on its own CEO: it refuses a message over 150 words or with a sentence over 30. The brand holds itself to the same rule.

---

## Positioning statement
For technical builders who want AI to carry real, multi-week work without babysitting it, **yaaf** is the open-source operating structure that runs AI agents as an accountable company. Budgets, deadlines, verification and vetoes are enforced by the server, not requested in a prompt. "Zero-human" platforms assume agents can be trusted. yaaf assumes they will overreach, and is built so they can't.

## Descriptions

**Short (≤160 characters):**
yaaf runs a team of AI agents like a company you chair. The rules live in the server, not the prompt. Open source, on your machine.

**Medium (about 50 words):**
yaaf is an open-source platform that runs teams of AI agents like a company. You set the goal and the budget. An AI CEO plans and hires specialist agents. The server, not a prompt, enforces the rules: work is verified before it counts, spending stops at the wall, and the date can't quietly move.

**Long (about 150 words):**
Single AI agents are capable but hard to trust over long work. They mark their own homework, drift and overspend. yaaf is built for the opposite case: work that runs unattended for days or weeks.

You describe a goal and yaaf argues with it until it is testable. It then hires a CEO and a team of specialist agents on the models and harnesses you choose: Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, OpenCode or yaaf's own loop. Every rule is a refusal enforced by the server. An agent can't mark its own work done, because the platform runs the test. It can't overspend, because every run is metered and the project pauses at the wall. It can't move the date quietly, because the date comes from measured pace. Critics can veto, and the CEO can't fire the last one.

You stay the chair. yaaf is Apache-2.0, runs on your machine, uses your own keys, and sends no telemetry.

## Tone rules
1. **Lead with the answer.** The first sentence carries the news.
2. **One idea per sentence.** If a sentence needs a semicolon, it is two sentences.
3. **Give numbers context.** "$412 of $500", not "$412". "14 verified, 0 self-claimed", not "lots of checks".
4. **Say what it can't do.** "It is not for quick tasks." Candour is the trust signal.
5. **Agents are seats, not friends.** Name roles first ("Compliance · Juno"). No personalities, no emoji faces.
6. **The human is the chair.** In public copy, use *chair*, which is gender-neutral. The product says *chairman*; consider aligning it (see `implementation-guide.md`, in this folder).
7. **No hype vocabulary.** If a competitor could say the sentence, rewrite it.
8. **Dry wit, rarely.** One understated line per page at most. Never at the user's expense.

## Language and phrases to avoid
- **Category hype:** zero-human, AI employees, autonomous company, digital workforce, AGI, superintelligent
- **SaaS filler:** unleash, supercharge, seamless, revolutionize, game-changing, next-gen, cutting-edge, magic, effortless
- **Over-promise:** guaranteed, never fails, 100% accurate, set and forget
- **Anthropomorphising:** "your AI team loves…", "meet your new coworkers"
- **Style:** exclamation marks, title-case headlines, all-caps headlines, emoji in product copy, "YAAF" in capitals, "Yet Another Agent Framework"
