# Writing the audit

The reader is a busy brand manager. He has his dashboards. After five minutes
he should think: *I could not have got this any other way.* That happens when
the audit tells him something true about his business he did not know, proves
it, and tells him exactly what to do about it.

## Shape

1. **The answer first.** Three things worth the most money a month, the one
   thing to leave alone, and the scale answer in one line ("about $25k more a
   month fits at today's ACoS; here is where").
2. **Your business, as the data shows it.** The portrait, and what good looks
   like for *this* business: the ACoS / TACoS band it can afford by job, beside
   today. This is often the most valuable page.
3. **Chapters**, each one claim-led and in money order. Each chapter has one
   takeaway sentence a manager could repeat to his boss, and the claims that
   prove it.
4. **The plan per tier**: moves, rollout, check-ins, stop rules.
5. **Leave alone**: what is working, what was checked, why not to touch it.
6. **Not covered** and **assumptions**: what the data could not answer and what
   would; what was assumed and what the owner told us.

## Sentences

- The thing that happens in his account, in his words: "two campaigns that beat
  your ACoS switch off every afternoon" — never a category name like "budget
  optimisation".
- The number beside every claim, with its count and window: "41 search terms,
  1,280 clicks, no orders in 60 days at your 11% conversion rate — chance of
  that by luck is under 1 in 1,000".
- Confidence in words: "very likely", "likely", "worth testing".
- Look forward. Not who set it up badly or how long it has run.
- Small is small. Never dress up a $40 saving.
- Never state a trend or number the data does not show.
- Insight beats volume. Five claims that change what he does beat thirty that
  restate his dashboard.

## What makes it worth $60k

- It knew what his business is and derived what good looks like for it.
- It found money he could not see (share gaps, under-invested acquisition,
  spend defending sales he already owns, a stockout coming).
- It proved each claim and said how sure it is.
- It told him what *not* to do.
- It gave a plan he can execute on Monday, with a way to know in two weeks if
  it works.
